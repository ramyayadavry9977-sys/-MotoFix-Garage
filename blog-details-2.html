How to Maintain Your Motorcycle Chain the Right Way

Learn how to keep your motorcycle chain clean, properly lubricated, and correctly adjusted for a smoother and safer riding experience.

MotoFix Garage Team
November 09, 2026
5 Min Read
Back to Blog Maintenance Tips

Your motorcycle chain plays an important role in transferring engine power to the rear wheel. Keeping it clean, lubricated, and properly adjusted helps your motorcycle perform smoothly and reduces unnecessary wear.

1. Clean Your Motorcycle Chain Regularly

Dirt, dust, mud, and road grime can build up on your motorcycle chain over time. Regular cleaning helps remove these unwanted particles and keeps the chain working smoothly.

Use a motorcycle-compatible chain cleaner and a suitable soft cleaning brush. Follow your motorcycle manufacturer's instructions and avoid using products that may damage the chain seals.

2. Lubricate the Chain Properly

Chain lubrication helps reduce friction between moving parts and protects the chain from premature wear. Use a lubricant designed for your motorcycle's chain type.

Apply lubricant according to the product instructions, usually after cleaning and drying the chain. Avoid applying excessive lubricant.

Maintenance Tip

Check your motorcycle owner's manual for the recommended chain cleaning and lubrication schedule. Riding conditions can affect how frequently maintenance is needed.

3. Check the Chain Tension

Incorrect chain tension can affect motorcycle performance and increase wear. A chain that is too tight or too loose may create problems while riding.

Check the chain slack using the measurement and procedure specified in your motorcycle owner's manual. If adjustment is needed, have it completed according to the manufacturer's instructions.

4. Inspect the Chain and Sprockets

Regular inspection helps you identify signs of wear before they become bigger problems. Look for visible rust, damaged links, unusual stiffness, and worn sprocket teeth.

If you notice unusual wear or damage, ask a qualified motorcycle technician to inspect the chain and sprockets.

5. Avoid Ignoring Unusual Chain Noise

Unusual clicking, rattling, or grinding sounds from the chain area may indicate that maintenance or inspection is needed.

Do not ignore unusual noises. Stop riding if you notice a serious mechanical problem and arrange for a professional inspection.

6. Follow a Regular Maintenance Schedule

Consistent maintenance helps keep your motorcycle in good working condition. Check your chain regularly, especially after riding in wet, dusty, or muddy conditions.

Always follow your motorcycle manufacturer's recommended maintenance intervals.
